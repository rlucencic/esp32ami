# The A1200 load survey on the Tab5, 2026-11-25

TODO item 17. Item 9's assembler waited on a title that falls behind. The
A500 survey (2026-11-11) found none below ~98%. The A1200 had one
profiled game, Aerial Racers' race, at 100% with core 1 at 71-78%.

**Answer: A1200 titles do fall behind.** 26 A1200 packages ran on the
board:

- 7 spent most of their run at 53-66% speed.
- 6 more had long stretches at 75-90%.
- 13 stayed at 93% or above.

There are two causes:

- **Wait loops.** Many titles spend 20-99% of their instructions in
  two-instruction wait loops. The poll skip doesn't cover these loops:
  they test RAM flags, VHPOSR outside its gate, or CIA registers.
- **Instructions that do work.** Other titles run 24-56k useful
  instructions a frame. The board manages about 15-20k a frame at full
  speed: the 68EC020 on Musashi costs ~1 µs of core 1 an instruction.

## Method

- **Titles.** The 26 packages that run as an A1200: `a-1200` and
  `a-4000` manifests. Each runs on its manifest's machine: Kickstart 3.1,
  2 MB chip RAM and the fast RAM the manifest asks for. The hard disk
  packages run with `workbench-311.hdf` or `workbench-135.hdf` beside
  them.
- **Host pass** (`host_pass.py`).
  - Each title runs for 15000 frames with `AMI_TIME` fixed.
  - The input is generic: clicks, fire, Space and Return at 4000 and
    7000, fire at 10000, then the joystick held in turns.
  - Seven titles have their own input and disk inserts
    (`titles.json`), for example Aerial Racers' fire and its disks 2
    and 3.
  - A picture every 250 frames, and the host's time for each 250 frames.
- **Board pass** (`board_pass.py`).
  - Each title restarts through the console's `R 3 7 2048 0 <fast KB>`.
    Its disks go in through the web API: a second floppy goes into DF1:
    as its own ADF, as on the host.
  - The same input goes in at the same frames. The console's `i` gives
    the exact frame. In the final pass most presses landed within 1-3
    frames of their target.
  - Four presses came 29-60 frames late, where the speed jumped and the
    wait from the fps lines overshot: Fatman's at 7000 and 14300, and
    Virtual Karting 2's click at 4000.
  - Speed comes from the console's fps lines (every 5 s). Core 1 comes
    from `/api/status`'s `board.load`, read once a second.
  - LCD pictures are taken at frame 9000, at the host's heaviest stretch
    (+500) and at the end.
  - At the first 3 s with core 1 at 99% or more past frame 3000, the pass
    takes a picture and a profile of core 1 (16000 samples). Titles that
    never got there were profiled at the host's heaviest stretch.
  - Titles the input doesn't get into play ran 10000 frames: Charr's
    menu, Scorched Tanks' Workbench, Diamond Caves 2, Overdose, Metal
    Kombat, Football Glory and Football Glory Indoors.
- **Analysis** (`analyze.py`, `pair.py`).
  - Speed is measured over each 5 s window past frame 3000 (the boot).
  - Core 1 is sampled outside the pictures and profiles: each takes a
    few seconds.
  - The board's pictures are paired with the host's at the same frames.
- **Instructions a frame.** Measured on the host with `AMI_PCLOG` over 50
  frames at each slow title's worst stretch, and at frame 9000 where it
  was in play. Each loop's code comes from `AMI_RING`, `AMI_STOP` and
  `AMI_STOPDIS`.

"Core 1 per instruction" is core 1's whole frame time (20 ms / speed)

20-24%). The chips cost most where a title shows 8 planes in a wide
fetch mode. The host and board took the same path at these frames
wherever their pictures match (below). For Tactical Manager 2 and Fatman
in play they differ in detail.

### The wait loops the poll skip misses

`poll_wait()` skips the rest of a slice for two-instruction loops on
DMACONR, VPOSR and INTREQR. `poll_vhpos_v()` skips VHPOSR's vertical
byte only for `cmpi.b #$F4` below $20000: broader gates broke Warm Up,
Ziriax and Tactical Manager 2 (`docs/poll-vhpos-experiment-2026-11-11.md`).
The loops found here:

- **RAM flags and counters**, set by the title's interrupt code:
  - `tst.w ($1742,A6)` / `beq`: Deluxe Galaga in play, 59% of its
    instructions.
  - `tst.w $d05ba.l` / `beq`: Soccer Kid, 82%.
  - `cmp.l (-$86,A5),D0` / `beq`: Starbase 13 70%, The Lost Prince 42%,
    Christmas Breakdown 31%. Three titles share this one routine.
  - `tst.w $2c2ee.l` / `bne`: Deluxe Pacman, 20%.
  - `tst.b $247c82.l` / `beq`, in fast RAM: Virtual Karting 2's race,
    13%.
  - `btst #4,$10043c.l` / `beq`: Fatman's title menu, 99%.
  - `tst.b ($a2,A6)` / `bne`: Sword, seen at a frame's end.
- **VHPOSR outside the gate**:
  - `cmpi.b #$64,$dff006.l`, then `#$F4`, at $0C501E: Black Viper's
    briefing. Together they take 99%.
  - `cmpi.b #$EC,(6,A6)` with A6 the chips: Fatman in play, 74%.
- **CIAs**:
  - `btst #0,$bfdd00.l` (CIA-B's ICR, a timer's flag), then the `#$F4`
    VHPOSR wait: Black Viper's race, 49% and 15%.
  - `cmp.b $bfe801.l,D0` (CIA-A's TOD, which counts frames): Zero
    Gravity, 8%.

### The rest is the 68EC020 itself

Without loops, Deluxe Galaga's instructions screen, Virtual Karting 2,
Leading Lap, Tactical Manager 2, Gea Lux, AmiBee, Charr and Metal Kombat
run 24-56k instructions a frame: a 68EC020 running from its cache or
fast RAM, never waiting. The profiles of core 1 (`board2/profiles.txt`)
show where the time goes:

- **Musashi's 68020**:
  - `m68k_read_immediate_16` is 17-36%: the instruction words through the
    cache model.
  - `m68k_execute` is 10-21%.
  - `m68ki_read_imm_16/32` take 3-9% more.
- **The chips**:
  - `display_fetch` 3-9%, `audio_line` 3-8%.
  - `memcpy` of the line records: up to 18% (Megaball's title), 13%
    (Charr), 12% (Leading Lap).
  - Collisions while a title reads CLXDAT: `matches16` and `collide` take
    9% in Deluxe Galaga.

## The harness's lessons

- **The console's `j` and `m` hold until the next one.** The title before
  left up and fire held, which sent AmiBee and Leading Lap elsewhere at
  boot. Both are released before each restart and at each title's end.
- **The runtime keeps a file in one drive** (esp32emu's `disks.c`).
  Putting the package into DF1: to get its disk 2 emptied DF0:. The boot
  then came from DF1:'s disk 2: Sword's Shell, Leading Lap's insert
  screen. Disk 2 now goes in as its own ADF, as the host has it. A user
  can't put an RP9's two disks into two drives either: TODO item 20.
- **The frame from the fps lines is too coarse.** Inputs timed from the
  5 s lines came tens of frames off, and Fatman then took another path.
  The console's `i` gives the exact frame. pyserial's 0.2 s timeout made
  each read wait for it, so the press came 5-16 frames late. With a 10 ms
  timeout it mostly comes 1-3 frames late. The pass still
  estimates the frame from the fps lines until 60 frames before a press.
  Where the speed jumps, that estimate falls behind the real frame and
  the press is late (four presses 29-60 frames late). Asking `i` all
  the way would fix it.
- **A picture or a profile takes seconds.** One taken between a press and
  its release held a key 105 frames. They now go where no input falls
  within 30 frames.
- **Keep a press's length.** A press sent a frame late and released on
  time is a frame short: Aerial Racers' menu missed a 4-frame fire, and
  the run never raced. A release now keeps the press's lateness.
- **Ruled out on the way**, while Fatman, Sword and Leading Lap differed:
  - the board's RP9 unpacking (tinfl against the host's zlib: the same
    disks);
  - the clock (`AMI_TIME` variants);
  - RAM across `R`;
  - stale written copies.
