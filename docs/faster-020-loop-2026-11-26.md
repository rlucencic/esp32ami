# A faster 68EC020, its own loop, 2026-11-26

TODO item 19, after `faster-020-2026-11-26.md`. That document found
that core 1 retires 0.62-0.78 instructions a clock wherever the code
lives, so the count of P4 instructions per 68020 instruction is the lever.
Its shares of core 1 (`pal`, the build committed then) in the titles that
compute: the fetch (`m68k_read_immediate_16`) 18-26%, Musashi's dispatch
(`m68k_execute`) 11-20%, the handlers 14-25%, Lisa's fetch 5-8%.

This one takes the fetch and the dispatch together in C first, before any
assembly: an instruction loop of the Amiga's own, with the opcode's fetch
inline. Then three smaller steps on top of it, each measured on its own.

The harness is the one before: the six A1200 titles over 15000 frames on
the Tab5 (the median speed past frame 3000, the slowest 5 s in brackets)
and the four A500 titles (core 1 over 20 s at a fixed frame).

## Step 9: the 68EC020's own loop

**Before.** Musashi's quick loop (2026-11-25) ran each instruction as: the
PC's odd test, the address mask, a call to `m68k_read_immediate_16()`
(the 68000 test, the compare with the next word, the cache), the jump
table, the cycles, the trace test. Counted in the committed build's code,
42 P4 instructions around a 68020 instruction whose opcode is a longword's
second word, 50 for a cached first word (the handler apart).

**Now** (`CONFIG_M68K_HOST_LOOP`, esp32emu; `-DM68K_HOST_LOOP` in the host
build): the quick loop hands runs of instructions to the machine's
`m68k_host_run()` (`mem.c`), which does the same with the fetch inline:

- Musashi's state through pointers it gives once (`m68k_loop`: PC, PPC,
  IR, the jump table and cycle table in use, the cycles left, the trace
  mark), loaded into registers for the run;
- the opcode at `s_ic.next` (the word after the last one fetched, even,
  so no odd test and no mask), its cache line looked at for a longword's
  first word, as before;
- anything else through `m68k_read_immediate_16()`; an odd PC goes back
  to Musashi, which raises the address error and calls again;
- the 68000 doesn't use it (`m68k_host_run()` returns 0: its prefetch
  stays in Musashi's loop).

28 P4 instructions around an instruction (a second word) and 36 (a cached
first word): a third fewer. The run stops where Musashi's would: the
slice's end, a trace mark (an SR write that sets T1 or T0).

**Checks.** `make -C ../esp32emu/components/musashi/test run-hostloop`
runs every Musashi check (interrupts, the disassembler, self-modifying
code, trace set and cleared by MOVE to SR and RTE in slices of 1 to 100000
cycles, T0 on the 68020) through a plain machine loop, 68000 and 68020; a
loop that doesn't stop for the trace mark or fetches odd PCs itself fails
them. The other Musashi projects (the ST, the Macs, the QL; Tab5 and
MaTouch) build with the option off.

## Step 10: extension words through a window (not kept)

The handlers read their extension words through `m68ki_read_imm_16()`, a
call to `m68k_read_immediate_16()` for each. A window
(`M68K_HOST_FETCH`): the machine kept `m68k_fetch.quick` at the next
word's address while that word was a longword's second (no look at the
cache needed), and the handlers read it inline from the machine's
pointer, every other word through the call.

On the Tab5 it made no difference beyond the runs' spread (table below:
Leading Lap 1.3 points down, Deluxe Galaga 1.2 up). In the profiles the
fetch's share fell by ~1.5 points and the loop's rose as much: the stores
that keep `quick` up to date on every word cost what the inline reads
saved. On the host Virtual Karting 2's stretch took 3.54 s against the
loop's 3.46. Taken out again, with its option in esp32emu.

## Step 11: branches within the bank

A taken branch leaves `s_ic.next` behind, and the opcode at its target
went through `m68k_read_immediate_16()`'s general path: the bank's wait
and host pointer looked up, the cache, the call. Gea Lux's play runs a
loop of four instructions and a branch: the fetch's share there was 17%
with step 9.

Now the loop keeps the bank of the last word fetched (`s_ic.bank`, its
host pointer and wait): a target in the same 64 KB bank is read inline
from there, with the cache's look as before. `mem_update_map()` (a new
memory map: OVL, autoconfig) and a CPU reset forget the bank.

In Gea Lux the fetch's share fell from 18.5% to 3.6%, the loop's rose
from 22.6% to 36%. Its slowest 5 s went from 84% to 89% in both builds
that have the step; Leading Lap's median 94% → 96%.

## Step 12: Lisa's fetch two words a step

`display_fetch()` copied each plane's words one at a time through the
chip RAM mask, hashing each into the line's key (FNV-1a: XOR, then a
multiply, one after the other). Where a plane's words don't wrap at the
end of chip RAM (all of them, in practice), it now copies two words a step
into two hashes with their own seeds, so neither multiply waits for the
other's; the old loop stays for a fetch that wraps. Both hashes are
combined at the line's end.

The first version hashed the two words as one 32-bit value. Nine of the
collection's games then differed from the old build. The words copied
were the same (a checking build compared them); the pictures were stale
lines: a multiply carries bits only upward, so a value's bit 31 only ever
flips the key's bit 31, and two such changes (two pixels) cancel. A line
whose key stayed the same wasn't drawn again. Two 16-bit chains have no
such bit.

`display_fetch`'s share of core 1 fell 1-2 points (Virtual Karting 2
8.8% → 6.5%, Christmas Breakdown 8.2% → 7.0%), and Virtual Karting 2's
race went from 74% to 77%.

## The stale sprite lines in Deluxe Pacman

Looking for those stale lines, a build that rewrites every record (the
truth: no key compares) was set beside ours at a picture every 100
frames over the whole collection. One game differed in every build,
before this work too: Deluxe Pacman's score table, whose 64-pixel
sprites hash `DATA << 16 | DATB` per word (`mix()`). The same flaw: a
change in a DATA word's leftmost pixel alone moved only the key's top bit,
and two of them cancelled, so a line kept an old picture of its sprite.

`mix()` now folds the product's top half back (`h ^ (h >> 16)`). With
both fixes the truth build and ours give the same pictures, 96 games of
96 at every 100th frame.

## A spoiled run

Two board runs were queued as two commands, the second waiting for the
first by a PID taken from `ps`. That PID was a short-lived subshell of the
first script: the second run flashed while the first one's survey still
held the serial port, the chip stopped in its download mode and the first
run's last title failed. Both were run again as one command, in turn.
Board runs go in one command from now on.

do (Musashi's effective-address helpers).
