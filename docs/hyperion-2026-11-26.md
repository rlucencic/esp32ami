# Hyperion's reads and the drive's speed, 2026-11-26

TODO item 21. Hyperion (Black Legend, 1993, an A500 with Kickstart 1.2)
stayed on "Loading... please wait" in most runs, and flipped between
loading and not after any timing change elsewhere. In the build before
this one it failed without input and with a key tapped at frames 200-215
in 14 of 17 runs.

## What goes wrong

The loader (at $6700-$6834 in chip RAM) reads a whole track at a time:

1. step the head or change sides, wait for the drive to be ready;
2. 15 ms on CIA-B's timer A ($2991 E-clock ticks);
3. DSKLEN $9A00 twice (6656 words with WORDSYNC on, sync $4489), then
   wait for DSKBLK;
4. decode: scan the buffer for `$4489`, skip any further `$4489`, decode
   11 sectors in buffer order into place, checksums on the way.

It has no retry: one bad read leaves garbage where the next track number
should be, and it seeks track $FFFF forever (the drive lines toggling
$70/$71 thousands of times).

The failing read starts with sector 0's header, no sync before it.
`AMI_DISKLOG` now logs the head's position at each DSKLEN: the write came
353.045 words into the track, 10 clocks into sector 0's second sync word
(sector s's sync words are words 352 + 544·s and 353 + 544·s, after a
350-word gap from the index). The first sync had passed before the DMA
was on, so it synced on the second, and the buffer began at the header.
The decoder skipped that sector, and its copy at the buffer's end is cut
off.

WinUAE and vAmiga behave the same at that moment (their disk controllers
compared for behaviour only): the sync is compared bit by bit, and only a
match after DSKLEN arms the DMA starts it. WinUAE's track from an ADF is
ours too: 6334 words (PAL), the 350-word gap first, 7 colour clocks a bit.

## Why the same read, and why so often

Each read's DSKLEN comes a fixed time after the previous read's end, and
that read began at a sync. So the reads land at a few fixed offsets from
the sector grid, a word or two apart from run to run: in two runs, the
same reads at +170, +402, +483, +521 words past a sector's first sync,
and so on. After a side change the interval is ~1661 words; across the
index the 350-word gap takes 350 of the 351 words past a sector boundary,
and the read lands 1-2 words after a sync: in the window. Where exactly
depends on our 68000's timing of the decoding to within one word (32 µs
of ~38 ms), which Musashi's timing isn't. With taps at frames 200-215 the
read landed at +0.53, +1.05, +1.21, +1.99 or +2.18 words, and failed at
the three inside [1, 2).

A real drive doesn't turn at exactly 300 rpm (specified to ±1.5%), so
the same interval spans a different number of words each time, and such
offsets spread over many words.

## The change

`floppy.c`: each turn of the disk takes its own speed. The word time is
224 clocks ± up to `WOBBLE` sixteenths of a clock (32: ±0.89%), drawn by
a hash from the turn's number (the drive's turns since power-on, mixed
with the drive's number). A reset doesn't start the sequence again, so a
load that met a rare race gets other speeds when retried. The host's
`AMI_DISKSEED=<n>` (`amiga_floppy_seed()`) gives another sequence, as
another drive would.

How often Hyperion's load fails, sampled fairly (each run with its own
speed sequence, `AMI_DISKSEED`, and the disk inserted at another frame,
so the rotation differs too; steady: 50 insert frames):

| Drive speed | Loads failed |
| --- | ---: |
| steady (before) | 23 of 50 (~46%) |
| ±0.89% a turn (kept) | 18 of 200 (~9%) |
| ±1.5% a turn | 35 of 200 (~17.5%) |

On the Tab5 (±0.89%): 3 of 23 boots failed (each boot a restart with the
disk in, the turns rolling on).

More variation is worse: it brings more reads within reach of a window.
The kept amount makes the failure five times rarer, not rare. A reset
retries with other speeds (and another rotation); a steady drive retried
from another rotation too, but at ~46%.

**A trap in measuring it**: the speed comes from the turn's number, and a
run from power-on reaches a given read after the same number of turns
whatever the input. Taps, other insert frames and resets moved the
rotation but kept the speeds at the reads that matter (one read landed
at 5.3 or 6.1 words in all 50 runs with other insert frames), and gave
17 of 17 and 39 of 40 at first. Only other sequences (`AMI_DISKSEED`)
sample the speeds.

## A DMA with no drive

The collection with the variation found a second problem. Cricket
Captain's loader screen started a raw read (trackdisk, no sync) right
after something polling DF1:, which is empty, had deselected every drive.
Our DMA advanced only with a drive's words, so it never ended. Paula's
DMA runs on her own bit clock, drive or not (WinUAE's read path without a
drive fills in bits too). Now, with no drive giving words (none selected,
the motor off, no disk), the DMA goes on at the word rate: a read gets
empty words, a write goes nowhere. Alone, that changes none of the
collection's 96 games.

Cricket Captain still hangs at that point with the collection's input and
the usual sequence: the garbage read is a race in its own code, which the
speeds decide. It loads with 40 of 40 other sequences, and without the
variation with all 17 taps.

## On the Tab5

The firmware with the variation, Hyperion (uploaded to the card as
`Hyperion.rp9`) on an A500 with Kickstart 1.2, 512 KB chip and 512 KB slow
RAM, the console's `R` for each boot: 20 of 23 boots reached its credits
by frame 4500; 3 stopped after 14 or 33 reads with the head at cylinder
83 (seeking track $FFFF). Speed 100%.
